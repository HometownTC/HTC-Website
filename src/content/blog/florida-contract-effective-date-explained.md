*MIAMI · November 26, 2026*

In the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar residential contracts, the Effective Date is the date the last of the buyer and seller signs or initials the final offer or counter-offer and delivers it. It is not the date typed at the top of the page. Every contingency and closing deadline counts from that date, so a wrong Effective Date makes every date after it wrong.

## Where the definition lives

The definition is in Paragraph 3(b) of the "AS IS" and standard residential contracts. The current versions are the [ASIS-7 and FloridaBar-7 (12/24)](https://www.miamirealtors.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2025/02/02-06-2025-Roadmap-of-Florida-Forms-Dec-24-Jan-25-New-Florida-Realtors-Forms.pdf). The 12/24 changes did not touch the Effective Date clause.

Florida Realtors' legal staff have summarized the rule this way: the Effective Date is [the date the last party signs or initials and delivers](https://blue-soho.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Law+%26+Ethics/3062032/489613/article.html) the offer or final counter-offer. Because of the delivery step, it "isn't always the date the last party signs."

## Signature is not enough. Delivery completes it.

Two acts are required: the last signature or initials, and delivery. A seller who signs at 9 p.m. Friday but whose agent sends the document Monday morning has not created a Friday Effective Date. [Lowndes](https://www.lowndes-law.com/newsroom/insights/florida-residential-as-is-contract-series-part-4-offer-expiration-and-closing-timeline) notes that many deadlines run from delivery, not from signing.

Paragraph 3(a) adds a second clock. A counter-offer can be accepted only within two days after the day it is delivered, unless the parties write in a different period.

## What "the 3-Day Rule" gets wrong

The phrase "3-Day Rule" does not appear in the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contracts. The contract's acceptance window for counter-offers is two days, not three. The Effective Date itself has no fixed waiting period. It is set by the last signature plus delivery.

## How the days are counted

Time periods are counted under the contract's Standards, in Paragraph 18(F). The contract uses calendar days, not business days. Since the [2021 revision](https://floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2021/09/florida-realtors-updates-frbar-contracts-and-riders), a calendar day is based on where the property is located.

If a period ends on a Saturday, Sunday or national legal holiday, it extends to 5 p.m. of the next business day. The contract points to [5 U.S.C. 6103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/6103), the federal holiday list, for which holidays count. Thanksgiving is on that list.

A holiday only matters if the deadline lands on it. Florida Realtors' [2018 guidance](https://blue-soho.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Law+%26+Ethics/3062032/489613/article.html) notes that a holiday in the middle of a period still counts as a day. For weekday deadlines, the law firm [Becker](https://beckerlawyers.com/do-the-deadlines-on-the-far-bar-as-is-residential-contract-for-sale-and-purchase-expire-at-500pm-or-1159pm/) reads the contract to run to the end of that calendar day.

## What rides on the Effective Date

The [default periods](https://www.lowndes-law.com/newsroom/insights/florida-residential-as-is-contract-series-part-4-offer-expiration-and-closing-timeline) in the "AS IS" contract are 15 days after the Effective Date for inspections and 30 days for loan approval, unless the blanks say otherwise. Deposit deadlines, the title evidence timeline and other contingencies also count from it. Shift the Effective Date by one day and every one of those dates shifts with it.

## How delivery can be made

Notices under the contract must be in writing. The [2021 revision](https://floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2021/09/florida-realtors-updates-frbar-contracts-and-riders) allows mail, personal delivery, fax and email. Text messages were removed as a notice method because they can disappear and are hard to prove.

## When the parties disagree

The contract does not include a separate procedure for fixing a disputed Effective Date. The answer usually turns on proof: who signed last, and when that party's acceptance was delivered. Email time stamps and e-signature audit trails are the usual evidence. If the parties cannot agree, the question belongs with the brokers and a Florida real estate attorney. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Effective Date the date typed at the top of the contract?

No. It is the date the last party signs or initials the final offer or counter-offer and delivers it.

### Is there a "3-Day Rule" in the Florida contract?

No. That phrase does not appear in the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contracts. A counter-offer can be accepted only within two days after the day it is delivered, unless the parties write in a different period.

### What happens if a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday?

It extends to 5 p.m. of the next business day. The contract uses the federal holiday list in 5 U.S.C. 6103.

### Can a notice be delivered by text message?

No. Since the 2021 revision, notices may be delivered by mail, personal delivery, fax or email. Text messages were removed as a notice method.
