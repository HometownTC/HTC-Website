*MIAMI · November 11, 2026*

"Ten days, no showings, no appointments" means a listing has drawn no buyer requests to see it in its first 10 days on the market. Agents treat it as a signal that buyers have looked at the price, compared it with other homes, and passed. In a shifting market, it is often the first sign that a price improvement, a lower asking price, is on the table.

## There Is No Official Rule

No Florida law, MLS rule or Realtor association sets a 10-day standard. It is field wisdom, passed from listing agent to listing agent. Agent guides repeat versions of it, from 10 days to three weeks, [usually without naming a source](https://www.effectiveagents.com/resources/when-to-cut-your-asking-price-the-data-backed-timeline-for-price-reductions).

The rule took hold because of how buyers shop. Buyers who are actively searching often get an alert the day a home is listed, so they see new listings fast. If none of them ask to see it, they have already made a judgment.

## The First Days Carry the Most Attention

Redfin measured this. In a study of [1.2 million listings](https://redfin.com/news/listing-views-new-vs-price-drop), homes got 3.4 times more online views on the day they were listed than on the day the seller cut the price.

Views fell fast after launch. They dropped to about half on day two and about a quarter after one week.

A price drop helped, but only briefly. A home 30 days on the market averaged 17 views a day. A price drop lifted that to 29 views, for one day. The next day it fell back to 18.

That is the math behind the rule. A listing's strongest week is its first. A quiet first 10 days means the home missed its biggest audience at its current price.

## The Market Has Shifted, Especially for Condos

Miami-Dade is now two markets. In September, single-family homes had [5.1 months of supply](https://www.miamirealtors.com/2026/10/16/miami-dade-breaks-annual-record-for-30-million-plus-home-sales-in-just-nine-months/), which MIAMI REALTORS calls a seller's market. Condos had 12.3 months of supply, which it calls a buyer's market. A balanced market is six to nine months.

Months of supply is how long current listings would last at today's sales pace. More supply means buyers have more choices, and they can afford to skip a home that feels overpriced.

Time to contract tells the same story. Single-family homes took a median of 41 days from listing to contract. Condos took 67 days.

So silence is not unusual for a condo seller waiting on a contract. But showings come before contracts. When a listing gets no showing requests at all in 10 days, the market is saying something specific: buyers are not even curious at this price.

## Why "Price Improvement"

Agents say "price improvement" instead of "price reduction." The home is not worse. The price simply becomes a better match for what buyers will pay. The new price also brings the listing back in front of buyers whose search filters stopped just below the old one.

## What's Next After 10 Quiet Days

A quiet listing usually comes down to one of three things:

- **Price.** The asking price sits above similar homes that buyers can see right now.
- **Presentation.** Photos, description or condition are not drawing clicks.
- **Access.** Showing instructions are hard to follow, or the home is hard to schedule.

The order matters. Activity data, such as views, saves, showing requests and feedback, shows which problem it is. Price is only one of the three, and the data helps the agent and seller decide whether it is the one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does "10 days, no showings, no appointments" mean?

It means a listing got no requests from buyers or their agents to see it in its first 10 days on the market. Agents read it as a sign the price, presentation or access is keeping buyers away.

### Is the 10-day rule an official standard?

No. No Florida law, MLS rule or Realtor association sets it. It is a rule of thumb based on how much attention new listings get in their first days.

### What is a price improvement?

It is a lower asking price. Agents use the term because the change makes the listing a better match for what buyers are paying.

### Is Miami-Dade a buyer's market?

For condos, yes, by MIAMI REALTORS' measure: 12.3 months of supply in September 2026. Single-family homes had 5.1 months, which it calls a seller's market.
