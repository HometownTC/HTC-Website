*MIAMI · November 10, 2026*

The choice between Florida's two FR/Bar residential contracts turns on repairs, not on the condition of the home. The Standard form obligates the seller to make certain repairs up to set dollar limits. The AS IS form carries no seller repair obligation, but it gives the buyer a right to cancel in the buyer's sole discretion during the inspection period. Both forms give the buyer an inspection period.

## The Two Forms

Florida Realtors and The Florida Bar jointly publish both contracts. The current versions are the [Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase (FloridaRealtors/FloridaBar-7x)](https://www.floridarealtors.org/media/5856) and the ["AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase (FloridaRealtors/FloridaBar-ASIS-7x)](https://www.floridarealtors.org/media/5857). Both were last updated [February 27, 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/02/fincen-rule-rider-updates-take-effect).

Florida Realtors also publishes a separate Contract for Residential Sale and Purchase, called CRSP. It is not a joint Florida Bar form and is not covered here.

## The Standard Form: Repair Limits

Paragraph 12 of the [Standard contract](https://www.floridarealtors.org/media/5856) gives the buyer an inspection period of 15 days if left blank. The buyer may order three types of inspection: General, WDO and Permit. WDO means wood-destroying organisms, such as termites.

Each type has a repair limit, which is the most the seller must spend. Each limit is 1.5% of the purchase price if left blank. The form says the seller only has to make the general repairs needed to bring items to the condition the contract specifies.

If repair costs exceed the General Repair Limit, a five-day window opens after the last estimate. The seller may agree to pay the excess. Or the buyer may pick which repairs the seller makes, up to the limit, and accept the rest "as is." If neither party gives notice, "either party may terminate this Contract," and the buyer's deposit is refunded.

## The AS IS Form: Sole-Discretion Cancellation

Paragraph 12 of the [AS IS contract](https://www.floridarealtors.org/media/5857) also sets a 15-day inspection period if left blank. During that period, the buyer may terminate by written notice if the buyer finds the property unacceptable "in Buyer's sole discretion." The deposit is returned.

If the buyer does not cancel in time, the buyer accepts the property's physical condition. The buyer also takes responsibility for any repairs the lender requires.

The seller still has duties. Paragraph 11 sets an "AS IS Maintenance Requirement." The seller must keep the property, including the lawn, shrubbery and pool, in its condition as of the Effective Date, except for ordinary wear and tear.

Florida Realtors calls this "a very strong right of cancellation" in its [contract law library](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/florida-real-estate-contract-laws).

## The Misconception: "As-Is Means No Inspection"

It does not. Both forms give the buyer an inspection period. Under both, the buyer pays for inspections and must repair any damage they cause.

What changes is the seller's side. Florida Realtors Associate General Counsel Meredith Caruso wrote in [Florida Realtor magazine](https://blue-soho.mydigitalpublication.com/article/LAW+%26+ETHICS/3431976/602939/article.html) that under AS IS, the seller has no duty to make repairs or even to answer a repair request.

She added that the buyer's right to cancel "is gone once the inspection period ends." A buyer who asks for repairs and waits for an answer can run out the clock.

## Side by Side

| | Standard (7x) | AS IS (ASIS-7x) |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection period if blank | 15 days | 15 days |
| Seller repair obligation | Yes, up to repair limits | None beyond maintenance |
| Repair limits if blank | 1.5% each: General, WDO, Permit | Not applicable |
| Buyer cancellation in inspection | Tied to repair costs over limits | Sole discretion |

## What This Means for Your File

As-Is doesn't eliminate the buyer's inspection period. It eliminates the seller's repair obligation. If the listing agent uses As-Is but the seller expects to negotiate repairs anyway, there's a form mismatch. That mismatch creates confusion at inspection, when the clock is already running. Confirm seller intent with the listing agent before writing the offer, not after. Monday morning, check every open AS IS file for an inspection deadline in the next 10 days.
