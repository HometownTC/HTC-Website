*FORT LAUDERDALE · November 12, 2026*

In Broward, you check open permits with whichever agency issues permits where the property sits. That is usually the city's building department, not the county. Broward County's Building Code Services covers only unincorporated areas and contract cities. Confirm the jurisdiction first, then search that agency's records for permits that were never closed.

## Step One: Find the Right Agency

Broward County's [Building Code Services](https://www.broward.org/Building/BuildingPermits/Pages/Default.aspx) issues permits "in the unincorporated areas of Broward County and cities we contract with." The unincorporated pockets are small. They make up the [Broward Municipal Services District](https://www.broward.org/MunicipalServicesDistrict/Pages/default.aspx), which includes Boulevard Gardens, Franklin Park, Roosevelt Gardens, Washington Park, Broadview Park, Hillsboro Pines, and Hillsboro Ranches.

Most Broward addresses sit inside a city with its own building department and its own records system. The Broward County Property Appraiser record shows which jurisdiction a parcel falls in.

## Step Two: Search the Records

Each city runs its own portal. Two examples:

- **Fort Lauderdale:** [LauderBuild](https://www.fortlauderdale.gov/LauderBuild) is the city's online records portal. Anyone can run a basic search by address, record number, or owner name without an account.
- **Pembroke Pines:** The city's [Development HUB](https://ppines.com/DocumentCenter/View/16350/CSS---How-to-Search-Public-Records-SOP) searches permits by folio number. It shows each permit's type, status, and inspection history, and can export the full history.

For unincorporated parcels, the county takes a written research request. Its [research packet](https://www.broward.org/Building/Elevators/Documents/BCSD%20Property%20%20and%20Building%20Code%20Violation%20Research%20Packet.pdf) lists a $50 fee and a reply in five to seven business days. Results cover county permits and building code violations only. The requester must follow up with other agencies, such as the city or the Clerk, for other liens.

A note on [ePermits OneStop](https://www.broward.org/ePermits/Pages/default.aspx): it is a shared portal for applying for permits across the county and its cities. It is not a public lookup of a property's permit history.

## Step Three: Read the Status

Status labels vary by agency. The county's research packet uses these:

- **Closed, Finaled, or Cancelled** — counted as closed
- **Issued** — the permit is active, and the work has not passed final inspection
- **Expired** — the permit lapsed before final inspection; counted as not closed
- **New or Plan Review** — the application is still in process

An "Issued" or "Expired" permit is what most people mean by an open permit. Either one can surface late in a transaction.

## Which Permits Cause Problems

Large jobs with several required inspections deserve the closest look: roofs, pools, and additions. An open permit usually means the job never passed its final inspection. Closing it out can require a new inspection, repairs, or a contractor who is hard to reach.

## What Florida Law Says About Old Permits

[Florida Statutes § 553.79](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/553.79) gives some relief, but it does not erase the issue:

- **Six-year closure:** A local agency *may* close a permit six years after it was issued, if it finds no apparent safety hazards. This is optional, not automatic.
- **New owners:** An agency may not deny a permit to an arms-length buyer, or penalize that buyer, only because a prior owner's permit was not closed. The agency keeps its remedies against the prior owner and contractor.
- **Expiration notice:** A local government *may* email or mail the owner and contractor at least 30 days before a permit expires.

## Title, Lender, and Disclosure

Title agents, lenders, and buyers' attorneys each have their own requirements for open permits. Ask them early what they need to see. Whether an open permit must be disclosed by the seller is a legal question for the seller's attorney, not the TC.

## What This Means for Your File

The permit search is the title company's job, not the TC's. Our part is tracking: we confirm the results are on the file and pass any open permit to the agent right away. A closing delay from a discovered open permit takes weeks, so the earlier it surfaces, the more time is left on the contract to deal with it. Monday morning: for every Broward file under contract, ask the title company whether the permit search is back, and note any "Issued" or "Expired" permits on the file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Broward County keep permit records for every property?

No. Broward County's Building Code Services covers only unincorporated areas and the cities it contracts with. Most Broward addresses sit in a city with its own building department and records system.

### How do I find out which agency issues permits for a property?

The Broward County Property Appraiser record shows which jurisdiction a parcel falls in.

### What counts as an open permit?

A permit with an "Issued" status (work has not passed final inspection) or an "Expired" status (it lapsed before final inspection).

### Can a buyer be penalized for a prior owner's open permit?

Under s. 553.79, an agency may not deny a permit to, or penalize, an arm's-length buyer only because a prior owner's permit was not closed. The agency keeps its remedies against the prior owner and contractor.
