*MIAMI · November 14, 2026*

A Florida broker must place escrow funds in an escrow account no later than the end of the third business day after receiving them. Sales associates have one business day to hand deposits to their broker. When a deposit dispute arises, the broker has firm deadlines to notify the Florida Real Estate Commission (FREC) and choose a way to settle it. Missing them is a disciplinary matter.

## The Deposit Clock

Florida law says a broker must "immediately" place entrusted funds in escrow, [under s. 475.25(1)(k)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All). [Rule 61J2-14.008(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61J2-14-008) defines "immediately" as no later than the end of the third business day after the broker receives the item.

The clock starts earlier than many agents think. Under [Rule 61J2-14.009](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61J2-14-009), a sales associate must deliver a deposit to the broker by the end of the next business day. Receipt by the associate counts as receipt by the broker.

For both rules, Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays are not business days.

The contract sets a separate clock for the buyer. [Florida Realtors notes](https://blue-soho.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Law+%26+Ethics/3137084/512771/article.html) that the FR/Bar contract counts calendar days, not business days, for when the buyer must make the deposit.

## Who Can Hold the Deposit

A broker's escrow account must be at a Florida bank, trust company, title company with trust powers, credit union, or savings and loan, [per Rule 61J2-14.008(2)(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61J2-14-008). The statute also lets the broker place the funds with a Florida title company instead of its own account.

The parties can also name a title company or attorney as escrow agent in the contract.

## When a Title Company or Attorney Holds It

The broker still has a job when someone else holds the deposit. Under [Rule 61J2-14.008(2)(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61J2-14-008), the licensee who prepared or presented the contract must name the title company or attorney in it.

The broker must request written proof that the deposit arrived. The request is due within 10 business days after the deposit was due. Within 10 business days of that request, the broker must send the seller's broker either the proof or written notice that none came. If the seller has no broker, the notice goes to the seller. This step is not required when the seller or seller's agent named the escrow holder in writing.

If a dispute arises, the FREC dispute procedures below are written for licensees holding funds. Florida Realtors general counsel has described a dispute over title-held funds as [a civil matter initially](https://blue-soho.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Resolving+Escrow+Disputes/2711755/384050/article.html), handled under the contract terms.

## When Buyer and Seller Both Claim the Deposit

A broker who receives conflicting demands, or who has a good-faith doubt about who gets the money, must notify FREC, [under s. 475.25(1)(d)1](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All). The broker must then pick one of four settlement procedures:

- Ask FREC for an escrow disbursement order (EDO), a written FREC decision on who gets the funds
- Mediation, with all parties' written consent
- Arbitration, with all parties' consent
- A court decision, including interpleader, a suit asking the court to decide who gets the money

[Rule 61J2-10.032](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61J2-10-032) sets the deadlines. The broker must notify FREC in writing within 15 business days of the last party's demand or of forming the good-faith doubt. The broker must start a settlement procedure within 30 business days of the same event.

Mediation has its own limit. It must succeed within 90 days of the last demand, or the broker must move to another option, under the statute.

## The Exceptions

Some deposits can be returned without these steps. The statute lets a broker return a deposit when a condo buyer cancels under s. 718.503. It also allows a return when a buyer, acting in good faith, fails to meet the contract's financing terms.

The safe harbor matters too. If the broker promptly uses a procedure and follows the result, no administrative complaint may be filed for the escrow failure, under s. 475.25(1)(d)1.
