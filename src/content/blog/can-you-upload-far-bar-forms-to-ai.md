*ORLANDO · October 26, 2026*

No. Form Simplicity's [Terms of Use](https://www.formsimplicity.com/terms-of-use/), last updated August 12, 2026, prohibit using its licensed materials with any generative AI, machine learning or automated content tool. That includes using them as prompts, training data or source material. Form Simplicity is the forms platform Florida Realtors offers its members, so the rule reaches most Florida contract files.

## What the New Terms Say

The ban sits in the "Restrictions" paragraph of the Intellectual Property section. It conditions the entire license on one prohibition: the licensee may not input, process or incorporate the licensed materials into any generative AI or machine learning service. The licensee also may not let a third party do it.

The terms list examples. Prompts, training data, fine-tuning data and source material for AI-generated outputs are all covered. The language says "includes, but is not limited to," so the list is not complete.

The penalty is spelled out. Form Simplicity treats any such use as a material breach of the agreement. A material breach is a serious violation that can end the contract. The terms also call it an infringement of the licensor's copyright.

## Who This Reaches

Form Simplicity is a Florida Realtors [member benefit](https://floridarealtors.org/tools-research/form-simplicity). The Pro Edition is free with membership, and the platform is a common source for Florida purchase contracts and addenda.

The rule is written into the software license, not into Florida Realtors' own [website terms](https://www.floridarealtors.org/terms). Those website terms already limit copying, uploading and sharing site content with third parties, but they do not name AI.

One detail is still open. The AI paragraph uses the term "Licensed Materials" without defining it. The safest reading is that it covers the forms and documents you produce through Form Simplicity. Until Form Simplicity clarifies, treat it that way.

## What It Does Not Cover

The prohibition is about the licensed materials. It does not bar you from using AI for general research, scheduling or writing your own emails. The line is the form itself. Pasting contract text, uploading a PDF or asking a chatbot to "read this contract" all put the licensed material into the tool.

## Popular AI Advice vs. the License

Much of the AI training aimed at agents shows AI reading the contract for you. One example is an October 2026 [LabCoat Agents guide](https://www.labcoatagents.com/blog/ai-real-estate-transaction-coordination/), which walks through AI pulling deadlines from a contract and updating calendars and CRMs. It keeps a human in charge of every step. It also tells readers to check whether their forms provider allows the use.

That last check is where Florida files hit a wall. Common advice says pick a tool that does not train on or store your documents. That protects your client's privacy. It does not satisfy the Form Simplicity license.

| The common advice | What Form Simplicity's terms say |
|---|---|
| "Use a tool that doesn't train on your files." | Training is banned, but so is any other use with AI. |
| "Use a tool that doesn't store your files." | Storage is not the test. Putting the form into the tool is. |
| "Let AI read the contract and pull the dates." | Using the form as a prompt or as source material is banned. |
| "Keep a human reviewing the output." | Good practice, but review after the upload does not cure the upload. |

In plain terms: a private, secure AI tool is still an AI tool. If a Form Simplicity form goes in, the license is broken.

## Why It Matters Beyond the License

Client data is the second issue. A purchase contract carries names, addresses, prices and financing terms. In a [September 2026 Florida Realtors article](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/09/agents-remain-responsible-ai-generated-work), a WAV Group partner reminded members that the license on the line is the agent's, not the software's.

The [National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/news/broker-news/why-every-brokerage-needs-an-ai-use-policy) advises brokers to block unapproved tools from touching client or transaction data. Lawyers face a similar standard. [ABA Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf) tells lawyers to read an AI tool's terms before putting client information into it.

## Three Questions to Ask Before Monday

1. **Does any tool in my workflow touch the contract itself?** That includes AI summary features built into email, storage or transaction software.
2. **Does my TC or assistant upload my forms to AI?** Ask directly. Get the answer in writing.
3. **Does my brokerage have a written AI policy?** If not, the broker is carrying risk no one has priced.

## What This Means for Your File

HTC has always held that technology supports human judgment. It does not replace it. This rule makes that position a license requirement, not just a preference. We do not upload FAR/BAR forms to any AI tool. We use AI for research, internal notes and organizing our own work. We never use it to process client contract forms. If your TC service uploads your contract forms to AI, ask how they comply with the Form Simplicity terms. If they can't answer, that's your answer.
