*MIAMI · December 15, 2026*

A real estate assistant supports your whole business, wherever the day needs help. A transaction coordinator (TC) owns one job: moving each pending file from executed contract to closing. Most Florida agents need the assistant when daily work is steady and the TC when work spikes under contract. Many busy agents use both.

## Two Roles, Two Different Problems

An assistant answers calls, keeps your calendar, updates your database, preps listing materials, and runs errands. Their day bends around whatever lands on your desk.

A TC's day bends around your contracts. That means building the deadline timeline, chasing documents, following up with the lender and title, and keeping the file complete for broker review.

| | Real estate assistant | Transaction coordinator |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Whatever the business needs today | Pending files, contract to close |
| **Usual pay setup** | Hourly wage or salary as your employee | Flat fee per file, often as a contractor |
| **Cost in a slow month** | Wages keep running | Drops with your file count |
| **Who builds the system** | You, together, over time | An established TC brings their own |
| **Best when** | Daily workload is steady year-round | Workload jumps when deals go pending |

Neither role replaces you. Negotiating, advising clients, and any task that needs a license stay with the licensee.

## What an Assistant Really Costs

The paycheck is only one line. Before day one, you spend time writing the role, screening, and interviewing. A first hire also means payroll setup and employer paperwork.

Once they start, wages run every week, busy or slow. In Florida, the minimum wage reached [$15.00 an hour on September 30, 2026](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution#A10S24), under Article X, Section 24 of the state constitution. That floor applies to every hour a covered employee works.

Then add training. A new assistant learns your systems, your standards, and your clients on your time. If they leave, that cycle starts over.

None of this makes hiring a mistake. A strong assistant can become the backbone of a practice. Just count your hours as part of the cost.

## Can You Pay an Assistant Per Closing?

Usually not the way you pay a TC agency. Two separate rules get in the way.

**Wage rules.** An assistant who works your hours, under your direction, is usually your employee. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee) looks at who controls how and when the work gets done. Federal rules let employers pay "per piece," but [29 CFR 778.111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-778.111) converts that pay into an hourly rate each week. If no file closes that week, the hours still count.

**License rules.** Florida limits how licensees can pay unlicensed people. Under [s. 475.25(1)(h)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All), a licensee can be disciplined for paying an unlicensed person for brokerage services. Pay tied to whether the deal closes makes that line harder to defend. We covered this in detail in [Which Transaction Support Fits a Florida Agent?](/resources/which-transaction-support-fits-florida-agent/)

Before you set any assistant's pay, talk with your accountant, an employment attorney, and your broker.

## Can Your Assistant Run Your Transactions Too?

Often, yes. The risk is focus, not skill.

An assistant's day is full of interruptions. A client calls. A showing moves. A lender needs a document now. A good assistant handles each one well.

Meanwhile, every pending file keeps counting down. Inspection periods end. Loan approval dates arrive. Association applications sit unanswered. None of it feels urgent until it is late.

A TC is built the other way around. Contract-to-close work is the whole job. An experienced TC also brings a tested checklist, so you are not building the process one file at a time.

## When Both Make Sense

The roles fit together. Your assistant keeps the business running. Your TC keeps each file on track. The handoff works when everyone knows, in writing, who owns which task.

**An assistant may fit if:**
- You need hands-on help every day.
- You have steady work to fill a regular position all year.
- You are ready to hire, train, and supervise.

**A TC may fit if:**
- Your workload jumps when a property goes under contract.
- You would rather pay per file than carry payroll.
- You want an established contract-to-close process from day one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a transaction coordinator cheaper than an assistant?

It depends on your volume. An assistant's wages run every week. A TC's flat fee follows your file count. Agents with uneven months often pay less per file with a TC. Agents with steady daily work may get more value from an assistant.

### Can my assistant be paid only when a deal closes?

Use caution. An employee must still earn at least Florida's minimum wage for every hour worked. Separately, s. 475.25(1)(h) restricts paying unlicensed people for brokerage services. Talk with your accountant, an employment attorney, and your broker first.

### Can an assistant and a TC work on the same file?

Yes. It works best when tasks are assigned in writing, so the assistant, the TC, and the agent each know who sends what and who approves it.
