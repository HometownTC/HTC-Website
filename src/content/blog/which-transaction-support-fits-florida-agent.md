*MIAMI · October 31, 2026*

Florida agents can get transaction support four legitimate ways: an in-house transaction coordinator (TC), a virtual assistant (VA), an independent TC agency, or a licensed Realtor working as a TC. Each route carries different costs, oversight duties, and pay rules. The biggest dividing line is the license. Florida law limits how anyone without a license can be paid for real estate work.

## The Four Routes Side by Side

| | In-house TC (unlicensed) | Virtual assistant (unlicensed) | Independent TC agency (unlicensed) | Licensed Realtor as TC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **License status** | None | None | None | Active Florida license |
| **How they can be paid** | Salary, hourly, or flat fee not tied to closing | Hourly or flat fee not tied to closing | Flat fee per file, not tied to closing | Through their own employing broker |
| **Brokerage tie required** | No, but works under your broker's oversight | No | No | Yes, one registered employer |
| **Oversight burden on you** | High: you hire, train, supervise | High: remote, often part-time | Lower: agency runs its own process | Shared with their broker |
| **Scales with volume** | Fixed cost; hire again to grow | Hours scale up or down | Pay per file | Depends on their broker's setup |
| **Training standard** | Whatever you provide | Varies widely | Set by the agency | License education, plus your process |
| **Watch for** | Tasks drifting into licensed work | Licensed tasks done offshore or unchecked | Success fees or commission splits | Being paid directly instead of through their broker |

## Deep Dive 1: How Unlicensed Support Can Be Paid

Florida law defines a "broker" by two things together. The first is the activity: selling, buying, renting, or negotiating real estate for someone else. The second is pay: "a compensation or valuable consideration," whether paid, promised, or expected, [under s. 475.01(1)(a)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All).

Doing that work without a license is a third-degree felony under [s. 475.42(1)(a)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All). Most other violations listed in s. 475.42(1) are second-degree misdemeanors under s. 475.42(2).

The paying side carries exposure too. Under [s. 475.25(1)(h)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All), a licensee can be disciplined for sharing a commission with, or paying a fee to, an unlicensed person for referrals or brokerage services. Section 475.42(1)(e) also makes that same conduct a second-degree misdemeanor. [Florida Realtors](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/compensation-commission) summarizes the general rule: a licensee may not share real estate compensation with someone who is unlicensed.

The statute does not name TC pay plans. A percentage of commission or a fee paid only if the deal closes is not automatically illegal on its own. But it ties the TC's pay to the outcome of the sale. That makes it harder to show the money paid for clerical work and not for brokerage services. A flat fee for defined tasks, earned whether or not the deal closes, keeps that line clear.

## Deep Dive 2: The Licensed Realtor as TC

A licensed sales associate working as a TC follows the rules for any associate. They may have only one registered employer at a time, [under s. 475.215(2)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All). They may not work as a sales associate for anyone who is not their registered employer, under s. 475.42(1)(b).

Pay must also flow through the broker. Under [s. 475.42(1)(d)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All), a sales associate may collect money in a brokerage deal only in the employer's name and with the employer's express consent. [Florida Realtors](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/florida-real-estate-licensing-law) puts it simply: when the work requires a license, the broker pays the assistant.

In practice, an agent at another brokerage cannot pay a licensed TC directly. The payment goes to the TC's broker, who then pays the TC. One narrow exception exists. FREC (the Florida Real Estate Commission) has allowed a broker to direct a closing agent, in a specific writing, to pay an associate directly at closing, [per Florida Realtors](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/compensation-commission).

## Deep Dive 3: Your Broker Still Answers for the File

No support model moves supervision off the broker. A broker can be disciplined for failing "to direct, control, or manage" a sales associate, [under s. 475.25(1)(u)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter475/All).

[Florida Realtors](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/library/florida-real-estate-licensing-law) also notes that licensees may not help, hire or advise an unlicensed person to practice real estate. If an unlicensed TC negotiates terms or advises a client, that conduct can reach the agent who hired them and the broker above that agent.

The practical test is the same for all four routes. Write down who does each task, who pays for it, and how the pay is figured. Then confirm that your broker has seen it.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

Whichever route an agent chooses, the same short list of questions applies. The answers belong in the written agreement, not in a phone call.

- Is this person licensed? If so, who is their registered broker, and does pay flow through that broker?
- How is the fee figured? Is any part a percentage of commission, a bonus at closing, or a fee owed only if the deal closes?
- Which tasks are in scope? Does any task involve negotiating, advising a client, or showing property?
- Who reviews the work? Does your broker know this person is on your files?
- What happens when a deal falls apart? Is the fee still owed for work already done?
