*WEST PALM BEACH · November 24, 2026*

A Florida HOA or condo association has 10 business days to issue an estoppel certificate after it receives a written or electronic request. An estoppel is the association's signed statement of what the owner owes. If the association misses the deadline, it cannot charge for that certificate. The rule is statewide, so it governs every Palm Beach file.

## The Response Window

For homeowners' associations, [Florida Statutes § 720.30851](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/720.30851) sets the clock at "10 business days after receiving a written or electronic request." Condo associations follow the same 10-day rule under [§ 718.116(8)](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/718.116).

The request can come from the owner, the owner's designee, a mortgagee, or the mortgagee's designee. In practice, the designee is often the title or closing agent.

The certificate must go out by hand delivery, regular mail, or email on the date it is issued. If the association misses the 10-business-day deadline, "a fee may not be charged" for preparing and delivering that certificate.

Business days matter. A request sent the Wednesday before Thanksgiving loses the holiday and the weekend from the count.

## The Fee Caps

The statutes set three base caps for a single parcel:

- **Preparation and delivery:** up to $250 when no delinquent amounts are owed
- **Expedited:** an additional $100 if the certificate is delivered within three business days
- **Delinquent account:** an additional fee of up to $150

Both statutes require the fees to be adjusted every five years for inflation, using the Consumer Price Index. The Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) calculates and publishes the adjusted amounts. [Florida Realtors](https://floridarealtors.org/advocacy/legislative-priorities/business-issues/estoppel) lists the current caps as $299 for preparation, $119 for expedited delivery, and $179 for a delinquent account.
## What the Certificate Must Show

Both statutes list required contents. The main items are:

- Issue date, owner names, and the parcel or unit address
- Regular assessment amount and how often it is due
- The date assessments are paid through, and the next due date and amount
- An itemized list of everything owed on the issue date, plus amounts coming due during the validity period
- Any capital contribution, transfer, or resale fee
- Open rule violations
- Whether the association must approve the transfer, and any right of first refusal
- Other associations the parcel belongs to
- Insurance contact information
- The collection attorney's contact information, if the account was turned over
- The signature of an officer or authorized agent

## The Validity Window and the Re-Request Trap

An estoppel does not last forever. Under both statutes, a certificate delivered by hand or electronically has a **30-day effective period**. One sent by regular mail is good for **35 days**.

If closing slips past that window, the closing agent needs current figures. That can mean a new request, a new wait, and a new fee. Confirm with the association what it will charge to update an expired certificate.

The expedited option buys speed for a fee. It does not change the validity window.

## What This Means for Your File

If a Palm Beach file has an HOA, the estoppel request goes out within 24 hours of contract acceptance. Waiting until the week before closing makes the estoppel the reason the closing misses its date. HTC's checklist puts the estoppel request in the Day 1 milestone group, ahead of inspection. The standard is the standard: Day 1, every file. Monday morning: list every open file with an HOA or condo association, confirm the estoppel was requested, and record the 10-business-day due date and the 30-day expiration on the file.
