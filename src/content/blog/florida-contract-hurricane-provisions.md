*MIAMI · November 21, 2026*

Florida Realtors does not publish a separate hurricane addendum. A named storm is handled by clauses already in the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar residential contracts: a force majeure extension, a risk-of-loss standard and an insurance-delay provision. An optional rider, Addendum H, adds insurance cost protection. None of these pause a deal on their own, so the details matter.

## Force majeure: the extension clause

Force majeure is a contract term for events no one can control. The contract's force majeure standard, Paragraph 18(G), names hurricanes, floods and extreme weather. It applies when a storm prevents a party from performing, or makes services, insurance or approvals needed for closing unavailable.

When it applies, deadlines, including the Closing Date, extend for [a reasonable time up to seven days](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2022/09/legal-hurricanes-transactions-and-contract-clauses) after the event no longer prevents performance. If it still prevents performance more than 30 days past the Closing Date, either party may terminate by written notice. The deposit then goes back to the buyer.

The trigger is prevention, not a forecast. Florida Realtors' [October 2026 guidance](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/10/florida-storm-threat-puts-transactions-watch) puts it plainly: "Don't assume a storm automatically pauses a deal."

## When insurance cannot be bound

Paragraph 5(b) ties closing to insurance. If a force majeure event makes services essential for closing unavailable, including [hazard, wind, flood or homeowners insurance](https://www.lowndes-law.com/newsroom/insights/florida-residential-as-is-contract-series-part-4-offer-expiration-and-closing-timeline), the Closing Date extends under the force majeure standard.

This is the provision most likely to come into play. Insurers commonly stop writing new policies when a storm approaches. Citizens Property Insurance, for example, [suspends binding](https://www.citizensfla.com/-/20250927-citizens-is-under-binding-suspension) of new coverage when the National Weather Service issues a tropical storm or hurricane watch or warning for any part of Florida. A [hurricane watch](https://www.weather.gov/wrn/hurricane-sm) means hurricane-force winds are possible within 48 hours. A warning means they are expected within 36 hours.

## Risk of loss: who pays for damage before closing

Under Paragraph 18(M), the seller carries the risk of storm damage until closing. If repair costs, including tree removal, are [1.5% of the purchase price or less](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2022/09/legal-hurricanes-transactions-and-contract-clauses), the seller pays for the repairs and the deal closes. If repairs are not done by closing, 125% of the estimated remaining cost is held in escrow.

If costs exceed 1.5%, the buyer chooses. The buyer may take the property with a credit equal to 1.5% of the price, or cancel and get the deposit back. Paragraph 18(L) lets the buyer ask for access to reinspect or do another walk-through after a storm.

## Addendum H: the optional insurance rider

[Addendum H](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/07/addendum-h-adds-flood-insurance-peace-mind) lets a buyer set a maximum premium for homeowners insurance, flood insurance, or both. If coverage at or below that cost is not available by the deadline, the buyer may terminate without penalty. The default homeowners deadline is 30 days after the Effective Date or 10 days before closing. It must be added when the contract is written. It cannot be added on its own when a storm is on the map.

## What the agents do when a storm approaches

Florida Realtors [recommends](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/10/florida-storm-threat-puts-transactions-watch) that agents review upcoming inspections, appraisals, walk-throughs and closings. Agents should check with lenders, closing agents, inspectors and appraisers about schedule changes. Buyer's agents should push buyers to stay in contact with their insurance professional. Listing agents should confirm plans for property access.

## Why the planning happens at contract signing

The Atlantic hurricane season [runs from June 1 to November 30](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/index.php). Choices that protect a client during a storm, such as Addendum H, realistic deadlines and early insurance quotes, are made when the contract is written. Questions about a specific file belong with the broker or the Florida Realtors Legal Hotline at 407-438-1409.

## What This Means for Your File

If a named storm is projected toward your county mid-contract, your timelines and insurance are exposed. Plan at contract signing during hurricane season, June 1 through November 30. HTC builds storm-season checks into file setup. Before your next hurricane-season offer goes out, ask your buyer to get a homeowners and flood insurance quote in writing. Then decide with your broker whether Addendum H belongs in the offer. If a watch is issued mid-contract, note the date on the file and tell every party in writing which deadlines may move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a hurricane watch pause a Florida real estate contract?

No. The force majeure clause applies when a storm prevents a party from performing, not when one is forecast. Florida Realtors' guidance: "Don't assume a storm automatically pauses a deal."

### How long can the closing be extended after a storm?

Deadlines, including the Closing Date, extend for a reasonable time up to seven days after the event no longer prevents performance. If it still prevents performance more than 30 days past the Closing Date, either party may terminate by written notice and the deposit goes back to the buyer.

### Who pays for storm damage before closing?

The seller carries the risk until closing. If repairs cost 1.5% of the purchase price or less, the seller pays and the deal closes. If they cost more, the buyer chooses: take the property with a credit of 1.5% of the price, or cancel and get the deposit back.

### Can Addendum H be added once a storm is on the map?

No. Addendum H must be added when the contract is written. It cannot be added on its own later.
